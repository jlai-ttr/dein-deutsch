GERMAN A1 — DAY 8 OF 90
Verbs: sein, haben, basic conjugation
======================================

Welcome to Week 2! Last week you learned greetings, numbers, and the
pronunciation cheat sheet. This week we begin grammar.

Today: the two most important verbs in German — sein (to be) and haben (to have).

WHY THESE TWO?
-------------
Almost every German sentence needs one of these. "I am happy" uses "bin".
"I have a book" uses "habe". Learn these first, everything else builds on top.

VERB 1: SEIN (TO BE)
--------------------

Conjugation in the present tense (Präsens):

| Person    | German    | English           |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| ich       | bin       | I am              |
| du        | bist      | you (informal) am |
| er/sie/es | ist       | he/she/it is      |
| wir       | sind      | we are            |
| ihr       | seid      | you (pl.) are     |
| sie/Sie   | sind      | they/You (formal) are |

Notice: NO ending for "ich" and "wir/sie" — only "du" gets -st, "er/sie/es"
gets -t, "ihr" gets -d.

VERB 2: HABEN (TO HAVE)
------------------------

| Person    | German    | English           |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| ich       | habe      | I have            |
| du        | hast      | you (informal) have |
| er/sie/es | hat       | he/she/it has     |
| wir       | haben     | we have           |
| ihr       | habt      | you (pl.) have    |
| sie/Sie   | haben     | they/You (formal) have |

Pattern: stem = "hab-", then personal endings -e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en.
This pattern applies to MOST verbs. Memorize the endings, not every verb.

EXAMPLES IN CONTEXT
-------------------

SEIN examples:
- Ich bin Student. (I am a student.)
- Du bist müde. (You are tired.)
- Er ist heute zu Hause. (He is at home today.)
- Wir sind Freunde. (We are friends.)
- Ihr seid spät! (You are late!)

HABEN examples:
- Ich habe Zeit. (I have time.)
- Du hast Glück. (You have luck / are lucky.)
- Sie hat ein Auto. (She has a car.)
- Wir haben Hunger. (We are hungry. — lit. "We have hunger")
- Ihr habt Recht. (You are right.)

CULTURE NOTE
------------
"Hunger haben" (to have hunger) is the German idiom. English says "I am
hungry" — German says "I have hunger." Same for "Durst haben" (thirst).
This pattern is common in German — emotions and conditions are things you
"HAVE" rather than things you "ARE."
