GERMAN A1 — DAY 12 OF 90
Indefinite articles (ein words) + Accusative
=============================================

Yesterday you learned the accusative for definite articles (der/die/das).
Today: indefinite articles (ein/eine) — German doesn't have "a/an" as separate words.

EIN/EINE BASICS
----------------
"ein" is used for masculine and neuter
"eine" is used for feminine and plural

| Gender    | Nominativ | Akkusativ |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| maskulin  | ein       | EINEN     |
| feminin   | eine      | eine      |
| neutral   | ein       | ein       |

Notice: same pattern as definite articles — only MASCULINE changes!
The rest stay the same in both cases.

EXAMPLES
--------
- Ich habe EINEN Bruder. (I have a brother.) — masculine, accusative
- Ich habe EINE Schwester. (I have a sister.) — feminine, accusative (no change)
- Ich habe EIN Auto. (I have a car.) — neuter, accusative (no change)
- Ich habe EIN Buch. (I have a book.)
- Ich habe EINE Tasche. (I have a bag.)

POSSESSIVE ARTICLES (mein, dein, sein, ihr...)
-----------------------------------------------
Same pattern — masc accusative gets -en ending:

| Person        | masc-Nom | masc-Akk |
|---------------|----------|----------|
| ich (my)      | mein     | meinen   |
| du (your)     | dein     | deinen   |
| er (his)      | sein     | seinen   |
| sie (her)     | ihr      | ihren    |
| es (its)      | sein     | seinen   |
| wir (our)     | unser    | unseren  |
| ihr (your-pl) | euer     | euren    |
| sie (their)   | ihr      | ihren    |

For feminine, neuter, plural — same in nominative and accusative.

EXAMPLES WITH POSSESSIVE
-------------------------
- MEIN Bruder kommt heute. (MY brother comes today.) — Nom
- Ich sehe MEINEN Bruder. (I see MY brother.) — Akk
- DEINE Schwester ist nett. (YOUR sister is nice.) — Nom
- Ich kenne DEINE Schwester. (I know YOUR sister.) — Akk (no change)

NEGATIVE: "kein/keine" (no, not a, not any)
--------------------------------------------
The negative of "ein" is "kein." Same ending rules.

- Ich habe KEIN Auto. (I don't have a car.)
- Ich habe KEINE Zeit. (I have no time.)
- Ich sehe KEINEN Mann. (I see no man.) — masc accusative

WORD ORDER: ACCUSATIVE IN THE SENTENCE
---------------------------------------
Standard word order with accusative object:
SUBJECT + VERB + (TIME) + (MANNER) + (PLACE) + ACCUSATIVE OBJECT

"Ich lerne jeden Tag (T) Deutsch (Akk)."
Wait — that's unusual. "Deutsch" is accusative here (what I'm learning).

Actually "lernen" takes accusative of what is being learned:
"Ich lerne DEUTSCH." — I learn GERMAN (Akk).

But "Ich lerne MEINEN Bruder kennen." — "I get to know MY brother" — two verbs.

VERB GOBBLES (common accusative verbs)
---------------------------------------
These verbs take accusative of what they act on:
- haben (to have)
- sehen (to see)
- hören (to hear)
- lesen (to read)
- verstehen (to understand)
- brauchen (to need)
- kaufen (to buy)
- suchen (to search for)
- essen (to eat) — what you eat
- trinken (to drink) — what you drink

Pattern: most ACTION VERBS take accusative of the thing acted upon.

2. Make 5 sentences with "Ich möchte ___." (I would like)
