GERMAN A1 — DAY 16 OF 90
Pronouns in Dative + Review
============================

Yesterday you learned dative articles (DEM/DER/DEM/DEN+n).
Today: dative PRONOUNS — what happens to "ich", "du", etc. when used in dative.

THE DATIVE PRONOUNS
-------------------

| Person        | Pronoun (Nom) | Pronoun (Dat) |
|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| ich           | ich           | mir           |
| du            | du            | dir           |
| er            | er            | ihm           |
| sie (she)     | sie           | ihr           |
| es            | es            | ihm           |
| wir           | wir           | uns           |
| ihr           | ihr           | euch          |
| sie (they)    | sie           | ihnen         |
| Sie (formal)  | Sie           | Ihnen         |

EXAMPLES IN CONTEXT
-------------------

Single dative pronoun:
- "Ich helfe IHM." (I help HIM.)
- "Das Buch gehört MIR." (The book belongs to ME.)
- "Wir danken UNS." (We thank ourselves.)
- "Sie hilft IHNEN." (She helps THEM.)

Pronoun + noun combo (possessive "mein" with dative):
- "Ich helfe MEINEM Bruder." (I help my brother.) — mein + em (masc dat)
- "Das Buch gefällt MEINER Schwester." (The book pleases my sister.) — mein + er (fem dat)
- "Ich gehe mit MEINEM Freund." (I'm going with my friend.) — mein + em

ACCUSATIVE + DATIVE PRONOUNS TOGETHER
--------------------------------------
When you have BOTH in the same sentence, there's a famous order:

DATIVE pronoun comes BEFORE accusative pronoun.

- "Ich gebe IHM (Dat) ES (Akk)." (I give him it.)
- "Sie schickt IHNEN (Dat) das Buch (Akk)." (She sends them the book.)
- "Er bringt MIR (Dat) einen Kaffee (Akk)." (He brings me a coffee.)

Mnemonic: "Dat-Akk" or "Ihm-es" (give HIM it) is the natural order.
Native Germans struggle with this in English ("give it me" → "gib mir es").

VERB GOBBLES (PRACTICE)
------------------------
Common dative-verb sentences:

- "Wie geht es DIR?" (How are you? — lit. "How goes it you?")
  Answer: "Mir geht es gut." (I'm doing well.)

- "Was fehlt DIR?" (What's wrong with you?)
  Answer: "Mir fehlt nichts." (Nothing's wrong with me.)

- "Das gefällt MIR nicht." (I don't like that.)
- "Wie schmeckt es DIR?" (How does it taste to you?)

These short phrases are everywhere. Memorize them.

DAILY QUESTION DRILL
--------------------
Answer each question out loud:

Q: Wie geht es dir? → A: ___
Q: Was hilfst du mir? → A: ___
Q: Wem gehört das Buch? → A: ___
Q: Wie schmeckt dir das Essen? → A: ___

PATTERN: ALL dative verbs follow the rule: who? what? receives the action.

NOUNS + DATIV — DECLENSION ENDINGS
------------------------------------
When you have an adjective BETWEEN article and noun in dative:

"der alten Frau helfen"
  der = article
  alten = adjective ending (same as article: -en for dative)
  Frau = noun

In dative, BOTH article and adjective end in -en or -em depending on gender:

| Gender    | Article (masc/neut) | Article (fem/pl) | Adj ending |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------------|------------|
| maskulin  | dem                 | —                 | -en        |
| feminin   | —                   | der               | -en        |
| neutral   | dem                 | —                 | -en        |
| plural    | —                   | den + n           | -en        |

This is "schwache Deklination" (weak declension) — the -en ending is everywhere after der/die/das/ein/etc.

EXAMPLES WITH ADJECTIVES
-------------------------
- Ich helfe DEM ALTEN Mann. (I help the old man.)
- Das Geschenk gefällt MEINER NETTEN Schwester. (The gift pleases my nice sister.)
- Wir gehen mit DEN KINDERN. (We go with the children.) [plural adds -n]

CULTURE NOTE
------------
"Ihm" and "ihr" are risky because they sound similar in some dialects.
  "Ich helfe IHM" (him) vs "Ich helfe IHR" (her)
In standard German, these are clearly different. In Bavarian/Saxony dialects, they might both sound like "am". Germans in those areas will know what you mean from context. Learners should focus on the written form.
