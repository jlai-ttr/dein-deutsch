GERMAN A1 — DAY 19 OF 90
Adjective Declension — The Infamous German Adjective Endings
============================================================

The topic every German learner dreads: German adjectives change their ending
based on case, gender, and number. But — there's a pattern.

THE THREE DECLENSION TYPES
---------------------------

1. STRONG (after ein/eine/ein or no article at all)
   Adjective takes the article's ending.
   "Ein alter Mann" — here "alter" takes -er ending (masc nom).

2. WEAK (after der/die/das)
   Adjective always takes -en or -e.
   "Der alte Mann" — here "alte" takes -e ending (masc nom after "der").

3. MIXED (after mein/dein/sein... or indefinite article)
   Adjective takes -en except in nominative masculine and nominative/neuter.
   This is for possessive pronouns (mein/dein/sein/ihr/unser/euer) and "ein" (when it means "a" not "one").

THE FULL TABLE (definite article — der/die/das)
-----------------------------------------------

|            | Masc | Fem | Neut | Plural |
|------------|------|-----|------|--------|
| Nom        | -e   | -e  | -e   | -en    |
| Akk        | -en  | -e  | -e   | -en    |
| Dat        | -en  | -en | -en  | -en    |
| Gen        | -en  | -en | -en  | -en    |

After "der/die/das", adjective endings are mostly -en or -e.
This is the easiest pattern.

EXAMPLES:
- "Der KLEINE Junge spielt." (The small boy plays.)
- "Ich sehe den KLEINEN Jungen." (I see the small boy.)
- "Mit dem KLEINEN Jungen spiele ich." (I play with the small boy.)
- "Das Haus des ALTEN Mannes." (The house of the old man.)

Notice: -en everywhere EXCEPT nominative masculine singular.

THE FULL TABLE (indefinite article — ein/eine/ein)
--------------------------------------------------

|            | Masc | Fem | Neut | Plural |
|------------|------|-----|------|--------|
| Nom        | -er  | -e  | -es  | -en    |
| Akk        | -en  | -e  | -es  | -en    |
| Dat        | -en  | -en | -en  | -en    |
| Gen        | -en  | -en | -en  | -en    |

After "ein/eine/ein" (or no article), adjective endings mirror the missing article.

EXAMPLES:
- "Ein KLEINER Junge spielt." (A small boy plays.) — er mirrors der
- "Ich sehe einen KLEINEN Jungen." — en mirrors den
- "Ein KLEINES Haus ist rot." (A small house is red.) — es mirrors das
- "Eine SCHÖNE Frau singt." (A beautiful woman sings.) — e mirrors die

THE FULL TABLE (possessive — mein/dein/sein/ihr/unser/euer)
-----------------------------------------------------------

Same as indefinite article (above).

EXAMPLES:
- "Mein ALTER Freund kommt." (My old friend comes.) — er mirrors der
- "Mein SCHÖNES Haus ist groß." (My beautiful house is big.)
- "Ich helfe MEINER ALTEN Mutter." (I help my old mother.)

"NO ARTICLE" CASES
-------------------

When there's NO article (just noun + adjective):

|            | Masc | Fem | Neut | Plural |
|------------|------|-----|------|--------|
| Nom        | -er  | -e  | -es  | -e     |
| Akk        | -en  | -e  | -es  | -e     |
| Dat        | -em  | -er | -em  | -en    |
| Gen        | -en  | -er | -en  | -er    |

Examples:
- "Kaltes Wasser ist gut." (Cold water is good.) — n + es
- "Ich trinke kalten Kaffee." (I drink cold coffee.) — masculine akk: -en
- "Mit kaltem Wasser wasche ich." (With cold water I wash.) — masculine dat: -em

PATTERN SUMMARY
---------------

The RULES OF THUMB (you can get 80% right with these):

1. After "der/die/das": adjective mostly takes -en or -e.
2. After "ein/eine/mein/dein/...": adjective mirrors what the article WOULD be.
3. In dative plural: always -en (regardless of article).
4. In genitive: usually -en (sometimes -es after some strong endings).

VERBAL REASONING:
- The adjective ending essentially communicates the case/gender that's NOT shown by the article.
- If you can see the article, adjective is just adding flavor (-en/-e).
- If you can't see the article (or it's "ein"), adjective carries the case signal.

DATIVE ADJECTIVE PATTERN
------------------------
After "dem" (dative masculine/neuter), adjective ends in -en:
- "Ich helfe dem KLEINEN Jungen." (I help the small boy.) — Jungen is dat
- "Mit dem SCHÖNEN Haus." (With the beautiful house.) — Haus is dat masc

After "der" (dative feminine), adjective ends in -en:
- "Ich gratuliere der ALTEN Frau." (I congratulate the old woman.) — Frau is dat

This is the famous "schwache Deklination" (weak declension) table again.

PRACTICE
--------

Translate these adjective + noun phrases (with correct endings):

1. The big house → das ___ Haus (groß) → "das große Haus"
2. A red car → ein ___ Auto (rot) → "ein rotes Auto"
3. The small boy → der ___ Junge (klein) → "der kleine Junge"
4. Of the small boy → des ___ Jungen (klein) → "des kleinen Jungen"
5. With a cold coffee → mit ___ Kaffee (kalt) → "mit kaltem Kaffee"
6. The nice woman helps → die ___ Frau (nett) hilft → "die nette Frau hilft"
7. Without a hot meal → ohne ___ Essen (heiß) → "ohne heißes Essen"

CULTURE NOTE
------------
German schoolchildren get this table tattooed into their brains from age 10.
Foreign adults usually catch it eventually but mix up dat/gen frequently. Don't panic.
