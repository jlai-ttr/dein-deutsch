DATIV — The Indirect Object Case
================================

Today we add the THIRD case. After Nominativ and Akkusativ, here comes Dativ.
Dativ marks the INDIRECT OBJECT — the recipient of an action.

"Ich gebe DEM MANN ein Buch."
(I give THE MAN [indirect] a book.)
  dem Mann = dative (recipient)
  ein Buch = accusative (direct object)

THE BIG SHIFT
-------------
For masculine + neuter, the ARTICLE changes in dative:

| Gender    | Nom | Akk | DAT       |
|-----------|-----|-----|-----------|
| maskulin  | der | den | DEM ✨     |
| feminin   | die | die | DER       |
| neutral   | das | das | DEM ✨     |
| plural    | die | die | DEN + -n   |

Masculine and neuter both shift der/das → DEM in dative.
Feminine (die) stays the same.
Plural gets -n added: "den" + n = "den Männer**n**"

Mnemonic: "DEM" = Dativ Emphatic Memory.
Think: "I give the man a present" — the man (DEM) receives the present.

VERB GOBBLES (DATIVE VERBS)
-----------------------------
Some verbs REQUIRE dative — the object is dative by default, not accusative:

  helfen (to help) — "Ich helfe DIR." (I help YOU = dative)
  danken (to thank) — "Ich danke DIR."
  gefallen (to please) — "Das Buch gefällt MIR." (The book pleases ME)
  gehören (to belong to) — "Das Buch gehört MIR."
  schmecken (to taste good to) — "Das Essen schmeckt MIR." (The food tastes good to me)
  stehen (to suit/look good on) — "Der Hut steht DIR gut." (The hat looks good on you)
  schaden (to harm) — "Rauchen schadet DER Gesundheit." (Smoking harms health)

These verbs ALWAYS take dative. Memorize them as set phrases.

DATIVE PREPOSITIONS
-------------------
A small set of prepositions ALWAYS take dative:

- mit (with) — "mit dem Auto" (with the car)
- nach (after, to) — "nach der Arbeit" (after work)
- bei (at, near, with) — "bei meiner Mutter" (at my mother's)
- von (from, of) — "von meinem Vater" (from my father)
- zu (to) — "zu meiner Schwester" (to my sister)
- seit (since, for) — "seit einer Woche" (for a week)
- aus (out of, from) — "aus dem Haus" (out of the house)

ACCUSATIVE-OR-DATIVE PREPOSITIONS (Wechselpräpositionen)
--------------------------------------------------------
Some prepositions can be EITHER accusative or dative depending on context:

- in (in/into)
- an (at/on, to)
- auf (on, onto)
- über (over)
- unter (under)
- vor (in front of, ago)
- hinter (behind)
- neben (next to)
- zwischen (between)

Rule:
  ACCUSATIVE when motion TOWARD ("Ich gehe IN das Haus" — going INTO)
  DATIVE when location AT ("Ich bin IN dem Haus" — already INSIDE)

"The two-way prepositions tell you movement (Wo? → Wo...hin?)"

EXAMPLES IN CONTEXT
-------------------

Simple dative sentences:
- Ich helfe MEINEM Bruder. (I help my brother.)
- Sie dankt DEM Lehrer. (She thanks the teacher.)
- Das Geschenk gefällt MEINER Schwester. (The gift pleases my sister.)
- Der Hund gehört DEM MANN. (The dog belongs to the man.)

Preposition + dative:
- Ich fahre MIT DEM Bus. (I'm going by bus.)
- NACH DEM Essen gehe ich. (After the meal, I'll go.)
- Das Buch ist BEI MEINER Mutter. (The book is at my mother's.)
- VON WEM hast du das? (From whom did you get that?)

Two-way prepositions:
- Ich gehe IN DIE Küche. (I'm going INTO the kitchen.) — motion
- Ich bin IN DER Küche. (I'm IN the kitchen.) — location
- Er legt das Buch AUF DEN Tisch. (He puts the book ON the table.) — motion
- Das Buch liegt AUF DEM Tisch. (The book is lying ON the table.) — location

CULTURE NOTE
------------
In casual German, people use dative everywhere. Hamburgers who learn dialect might say "Ich geh zu meine Mutter" (correctly: "zu meiner Mutter"). You WILL hear informal dative in real life. Both forms are heard. The polite/standard form always uses correct case.
